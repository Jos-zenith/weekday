# Weekday Scheduling Desk

My take on the Weekday scheduling brief: about 500 interview requests land every evening, and two interns (Ram and Shyam) have to get 80% of them booked within 24 hours, using a process simple enough to follow without judgement calls.

> **Please read first: everything here is synthetic.**
> - **The data is made up.** Every candidate, phone number, email address and partner in this repo was generated by `analysis/simulation.py`. Emails use `example.com`, phone numbers are placeholder patterns, and no real person or company appears.
> - **The rates are assumptions, not measurements.** First-try booking rate, the share who book without a call, how fast later tries fall off, minutes per try, the hire rate, the fee and the share of late requests that are never interviewed are all my planning values.

## summary

**The problem.** At about 19:51 each evening, 500 requests arrive from 100 partners. Nobody can be called before 10:00, so 14 of the 24 hours are unusable, and the callers are two interns.

**The finding.** Two interns cannot reach 80% by phone. Before the deadline they have time for about **210 tries**, and about **426 people** sit behind the 500 requests (1.17 requests each). Even if every single call ended in a booking, interns would book at most about **49%** of requests. My working estimate is about **45%**. Every share is a share of the 500 requests.

**What 80% takes** Candidates booking on their own, more people, or both. At my planning values, 80% needs at least 31% of requests booked with no call at all. Whether more people would even help depends on numbers I can only get from two live days, and the plan says what to measure and what to do with the answer.

**What I built.** A sheet where interns make no judgement calls: a ranked list, one instruction per row, a WhatsApp message already typed, the partner's booking link, and one log line per conversation. Each request keeps an ID (R001, R002, ...) from the partner's sheet to the booking. A sent booking link never counts as a booking.

**What I need.** The partner's written OK to book on their calendars, WhatsApp opt-in collected when candidates say yes, and openness to resetting the target if the data says 80% cannot be bought.

**Why it matters, as a scenario and not a measured loss.** At my planning values, the gap puts roughly Rs 3.6 lakh of expected fee value at risk per day, and Rs 14.3 lakh in the upper case. Both rest on the assumptions below.

## The assumptions

One assumption set is used everywhere: the plan, `analysis/scenarios.py` and the DASHBOARD model in the workbook. Each is the first thing to replace with measured data.

| Assumption | Planning value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Share of requests booked with no call | 25% | Tested at 15%, 25% and 40% |
| First-try confirmed-booking rate | 40% | Tested at 30% and 40% |
| Each later try keeps this share of the previous rate | 0.7 | Tested at 1.0, 0.7 and 0.4 |
| Requests settled per successful call | 1.17 | Derived from the made-up batch; the simulated day gave 1.15 |
| Minutes per try | 4.5 | First thing to measure live |
| Intern hours | Ram 10:00-18:00, Shyam 12:00 to the deadline | 475 minutes each before 19:51 |
| Late requests that are never interviewed | 25% | My least certain input |
| Fee value | Rs 18 lakh CTC x 15% fee x 3% hire rate, about Rs 8,100 per interview | Assumes the hire rate holds for marginal requests |
| Cost of an extra intern | About Rs 900 a day (Rs 20,000 over 22 working days) | Used in the cost sensitivity |

## How the sheet is laid out

- **Interns:** START HERE, RAM, SHYAM and LOG. Each intern gets a ranked list with one instruction per row, a typed WhatsApp message and the partner's booking link. They log one line per conversation.
- **Lead:** DASHBOARD, MESSAGES (for piloting an evening WhatsApp), PARTNERS, INPUT and SETTINGS.
- **Hidden:** DESK (the engine) and LISTS (outcome codes and message text).

## What's in this repo

| Path | What it is |
|---|---|
| `Weekday Scheduling Plan.pdf` | The written plan. |
| `Weekday Scheduling Desk.xlsx` | The interns' sheet, with demo data. |
| `analysis/scenarios.py` | The "what would 80% take" table, the capacity ceiling and its outer bounds, the fee scenario and the cost sensitivity. Plain arithmetic, no randomness, so any number can be checked by hand. Writes `scenarios.json`. |
| `analysis/simulation.py` | A simulation of candidate behaviour. Here it only generates the demo data. |
| `tests/request_tracking.py` | 29 checks that each request keeps its ID through repeated rounds, several requests per person, duplicates and partial answers. |
| `tests/model_consistency.py` | Rebuilds the ceiling and its bounds from the raw demo batch, then checks the DASHBOARD model against `analysis/scenarios.py` for seven scenarios and reports any gap. |
| `tests/dry_run.py` | A bot works one simulated day through the sheet. The pass/fail checks are what matter; the booking numbers it produces come from the assumptions above. |
| `tests/results/` | Output of the last test runs, including `demo-candidates.json` (how each made-up candidate behaves). |

To re-run: `python analysis/scenarios.py` needs only the Python standard library. The three scripts in `tests/` drive the workbook through desktop Excel using a small helper, `tests/excel.py`, and also need `numpy` and `openpyxl`.

## Going live

1. In SETTINGS, clear the test clock (B3). Until it's blank, a red TEST CLOCK warning shows on START HERE, RAM, SHYAM and DASHBOARD.
2. In INPUT, delete the demo rows and paste the partner sheet into columns A-I. Column K gives each request its ID; paste the partner's own IDs over it if they have them. After interns start logging, only add rows at the bottom and don't sort.
3. In PARTNERS, add each company's booking link. Only mark "We may book for candidates?" as YES once the partner has agreed in writing.
4. Real candidate data is personal data. Keep the live workbook out of this repo.
